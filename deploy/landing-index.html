Provenance

From the hot shop to the archive.

The making is live and fleeting; the record shouldn't be. Glowtbook is a working bench journal and object registry — log a piece, photograph it off the pipe, and record where it's been. Installs as an app on your phone.

Open Glowtbook

What travels with a piece

  • A preservation-grade archive of the originals (BagIt / OAIS)
  • Content Credentials (C2PA) signed into the image
  • A re-identification fingerprint to confirm a physical piece later
  • Structured style traits aligned to Getty AAT & Wikidata
  • A listing built to be screen-reader accessible from the start

Experimental

A digital barcode for one-of-a-kind glass.

Every mass-produced thing has a barcode; a handmade piece has none — so it can't be told from a copy, or reunited with its record if it's sold, lent, or lost. Shoot a piece from every angle on a printed mat and the app turns it into a fingerprint unique to that physical object — a barcode you don't print, you read off the glass. Anyone can later confirm this is the same piece, in a browser.

Backed by Content Credentials (C2PA)

The barcode is bound to tamper-evident provenance — a compact attestation, signed into the piece's C2PA Content Credentials — so a verified match isn't just "looks alike"; it's anchored to a signed record of what the piece is and when it was enrolled. This is experimental: it works today for enrol and verify, and it's strongest on pieces with distinctive colour and detail. Treat a result as strong evidence, not forensic proof.

Open shop documentation

Describe a shop through its components.

A hot shop is a system — gas, air, power, heat, exhaust, and the controls that tie it together. Document yours as diagrams and a bill of materials with real part numbers so the next person can fix, order, or build one. Describe it to an AI, have it render the diagrams, and publish it here.
