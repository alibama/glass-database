Museum-grade cataloging, for everyone

When you contribute, the full-quality original and any private values stay with you. A condensed, public copy goes to the registry, carrying:

  • a preservation-grade archive of the originals (BagIt / OAIS)
  • Content Credentials (C2PA) signed into the image
  • a re-identification fingerprint to confirm a physical piece later
  • structured style traits aligned to Getty AAT & Wikidata
  • a listing built to be screen-reader accessible from the start

Standards museums use (SKOS, CIDOC-CRM, C2PA) — so a studio, a school, or a collection can all speak the same language.

Experimental

A digital barcode for one-of-a-kind glass

Every mass-produced thing has a barcode. A handmade piece has none — so it can't be told apart from a copy, or reunited with its record if it's sold, lent, or lost. We're changing that: shoot a piece from every angle on a printed mat and the app turns it into a fingerprint unique to that physical object — a barcode you don't print, you read off the glass. Anyone can later confirm this is the same piece, right in a browser.

Backed by Content Credentials (C2PA)

The barcode is bound to tamper-evident provenance: a compact attestation — rating, colour, and a hash of the fingerprint — is signed into the piece's C2PA Content Credentials, the same standard used to prove where a photo came from. So a verified match isn't just "looks alike"; it's anchored to a signed record of what the piece is and when it was enrolled.

This is experimental. It works today — you can enrol a piece and verify it — and it's strongest on pieces with distinctive colour and detail; clear glass and precise measurement are still being refined. Treat a result as strong evidence, not forensic proof. Turn on the optional on-device AI (DINOv2) to tell similar-coloured pieces apart.
