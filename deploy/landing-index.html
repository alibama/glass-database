№ 03 Field notes

On the business of glass.

What a hot shop costs to build and run, where the buyers are, and what the data says. Studies and essays, published under CC BY.

Latest · Theoretical studies · Oct 17, 2026 · 21 min read

Blowing Glass in Lima

Lima is an easy place to melt glass and a hard place to sell it — cheap piped gas, a mining economy that digs up every oxide in a glass batch, and a city of eleven million buying its glass from somewhere else. A hot shop here costs about $380,000 to build and $350,000 a year to run, and the constraint is not energy but demand.

№ 04 Provenance · Glowtbook

From the hot shop to the archive.

The making is fleeting; the record shouldn’t be. Glowtbook is a bench journal and object registry: log a piece, photograph it off the pipe, record where it’s been. Installs on your phone.

Open GlowtbookSeasons

What travels with a piece

  • i.A preservation-grade archive of the originals (BagIt / OAIS)
  • ii.Content Credentials (C2PA) signed into the image
  • iii.A re-identification fingerprint to confirm the physical piece later
  • iv.Style traits aligned to Getty AAT & Wikidata
  • v.A listing that’s screen-reader accessible from the start

№ 05 Barcoding · Alpha — for testers

A digital barcode for one-of-a-kind glass.

A handmade piece has no barcode, so it can’t be told from a copy or reunited with its record. Orbit it on a printed mat and the app reads a fingerprint off the glass itself. Anyone can later confirm this is the same piece, in a browser.

Sign in once, or pair your phone from Glowtbook with a QR, pick the piece, and orbit it until the rating reads Strong. The printable mat gives true scale and colour.

Every angle becomes one fingerprint, saved straight onto the piece in your registry, not lost in an export. An unsaved session is kept on the device until you save it.

Verify on the spot, then publish with the fingerprint signed into its C2PA Content Credentials, a tamper-evident record of what the piece is and when it was enrolled.

Alpha, open to testers. Enrol and verify work today and are strongest on pieces with distinctive colour and detail; thresholds are still being calibrated on real pieces. Treat a match as strong evidence, not forensic proof. If you have a shelf of your own work and a phone, you’re exactly who we need.

№ 08 About

Craft and capital, in one open record.

The Glass Database was started by Jason Bauer to look at the glass world from both sides at once: the craft and the capital. What a studio costs to build and run, who taught whom, where the work goes and what it sells for. It picked up attention, and it is now an open-source project open to glassblowers, collectors and researchers interested in the emerging, evolving space of studio art glass.

Founder

Jason Robert Bauer

Artist and founder of the Glass Database (b. 1986). Trained as a furnace glassmaker and a lifelong craft enthusiast, he works mainly with light, and also with sound and time, through a futurist aesthetic designed to shift the viewer’s perception. He works intuitively, often building from parts made on separate occasions. With his wife and partner Romina Gonzales he co-founded J & R Bauer Inc. and The Third Room. Based between north-western New York and western Peru.

Co-founder · maintainer

Anson Parker

Keeps the furnace running at Raging Buffalo Glass and has spent almost thirty years building open-source software for libraries. The registry’s code, the capture app, the API and the archive plumbing are his.

How it’s run

  • i.Data under CC BY 4.0, with attribution kept and removal on request; code under Apache 2.0 on GitHub.
  • ii.Nothing is public until it’s reviewed: every row passes an approval gate, and names and birth dates stay out of the per-row views.
  • iii.Standards where they exist: C2PA for credentials, BagIt and OAIS for the archive, SKOS aligned to Getty AAT and Wikidata for style.
  • iv.Conversation happens on the Discord; the roadmap and changelog live in the repository.
