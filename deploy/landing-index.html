Blowing Glass in Lima

Lima is an easy place to melt glass and a hard place to sell it — cheap piped gas, a mining economy that digs up every oxide in a glass batch, and a city of eleven million buying its glass from somewhere else. A hot shop here costs about $380,000 to build and $350,000 a year to run, and the constraint is not energy but demand.

Provenance

From the hot shop to the archive.

The making is live and fleeting; the record shouldn't be. Glowtbook is a working bench journal and object registry — log a piece, photograph it off the pipe, and record where it's been. Installs as an app on your phone.

Open Glowtbook

What travels with a piece

  • A preservation-grade archive of the originals (BagIt / OAIS)
  • Content Credentials (C2PA) signed into the image
  • A re-identification fingerprint to confirm a physical piece later
  • Structured style traits aligned to Getty AAT & Wikidata
  • A listing built to be screen-reader accessible from the start

Glowtbook · Barcoding · alpha — for testers

A digital barcode for one-of-a-kind glass.

Every mass-produced thing has a barcode; a handmade piece has none — so it can't be told from a copy, or reunited with its record if it's sold, lent, or lost. Shoot a piece from every angle on a printed mat and the app turns it into a fingerprint unique to that physical object — a barcode you don't print, you read off the glass. Anyone can later confirm this is the same piece, in a browser.

Backed by Content Credentials (C2PA)

The barcode is bound to tamper-evident provenance — a compact attestation, signed into the piece's C2PA Content Credentials — so a verified match isn't just "looks alike"; it's anchored to a signed record of what the piece is and when it was enrolled. This is an alpha, open to testers: enrol and verify work today and are strongest on pieces with distinctive colour and detail, thresholds are still being calibrated on real pieces, and a result is strong evidence, not forensic proof. If you have a shelf of your own work and a phone, you're exactly who we need.

Sign in once

The capture app knows who you are — sign in with Google, or pair your phone from Glowtbook with a QR code and it opens already signed in, with the piece chosen.

Save, don't export

Pick the piece you're fingerprinting and the capture saves straight onto it in your registry; an unsaved session is kept on the device until you do.

Then verify, publish, play

Verify it on the spot, publish it with the fingerprint signed into its credentials, and enter it in the current season.

About

Craft and capital, in one open record.

The Glass Database was started by Jason Bauer to look at the glass world from both sides at once: the craft and the capital. What a studio costs to build and run, who taught whom, where the work goes and what it sells for. The project picked up attention, and it is now an open-source project open to glassblowers, collectors and researchers interested in the emerging, evolving space of studio art glass. Data is CC BY 4.0, the code is Apache 2.0, and anyone can add to it.

Founder

Jason Robert Bauer

Artist and founder of the Glass Database (b. 1986). Trained as a furnace glassmaker and a lifelong craft enthusiast, he works mainly with light, and also with sound and time, through a futurist aesthetic designed to shift the viewer's perception. He works intuitively, often building from parts made on separate occasions. With his wife and partner Romina Gonzales he co-founded J & R Bauer Inc. and The Third Room. He is based between north-western New York and western Peru.

Co-founder · maintainer

Anson Parker

Keeps the furnace running at Raging Buffalo Glass and has spent almost thirty years building open-source software for libraries. The registry's code, the capture app, the API and the archive plumbing are where he hacks on it; it is an open-source project and he is glad to.
