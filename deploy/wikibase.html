    № 01 Wikibase

    The record, on the wiki.

    Everything public and durable in the Glass Database is moving to glass.wikibase.cloud, where the Museum of Glass and anyone else can cite it, correct it and carry it on to Wikidata. This page is the migration itself: every step, every item, every run, as it happens.

    № 02 The pipeline

    Eight steps, in order.

    Stage the tables into items, fold the duplicates, reconcile names in OpenRefine, create the properties, rehearse, push, then read back what the wiki holds. Each step says where it stands; administrators get the button.

      № 03 What goes where

      Public and durable goes to the wiki.

      One test decides it: if a fact is public, durable and useful to someone who is not us, it belongs on the wiki. If it is private, changes by the minute or weighs megabytes, it stays in the app.

      In the wiki

      People who make glass and the organisations around them, the published pieces with everything their public page shows, the vocabularies, lineage, shows and sales, collecting events and their results, awards, and the links to Wikidata and Getty that make the record citable.

      Every item carries a Glass Database ID, so a wiki item and our row always find each other.

      In the app

      Emails, phones and demographic fields, prices paid and buyers, votes while a season runs, claims and their reports, event registrations, unpublished pieces and their images, fingerprint vectors and frames, the archive bags, sessions and analytics.

      And a cache of the wiki, so the pages never wait on a query service.

      Collectors

      Collectors are captured on the site when they check a piece and add it to their collection. They stay in the app: no collector item, no collection, no claim and no vote is ever staged. A piece on the wiki shows no owner.

      A collector who later wants a public profile can have a person item of their own, with none of their collection on it.

      № 05 Duplicates

      One item per thing.

      The same person in the artist list and the legacy sheet, the same studio in the directory and a submission, the same city spelt two ways. Each cluster keeps one canonical item; the others fold into it and keep pointing at it through their own Glass Database IDs. If two really are different, keep them apart.

      No clusters yet. Run Stage; it finds duplicates on the way.

      № 06 OpenRefine

      Names to Wikidata, in OpenRefine.

      The wiki can hold a person without a Wikidata id, and the id arrives as a statement when it is found. OpenRefine does the finding: its built-in Wikidata reconciliation for people, studios, schools and places, and this site's own reconciliation service for what is already staged or on the wiki.

      1. Download a kind below (people, studios, places, schools…). The CSV has a key per row, the label, description, aliases and the properties staging built.
      2. In OpenRefine, reconcile the label column against Wikidata (the type it suggests, or Q5 for people). Copy the match into wikidata_qid: cell.recon.match.id.
      3. To check against what is already here, add a reconciliation service with this URL: . The OpenRefine Wikibase extension's manifest (after Bootstrap): .
      4. Fix labels, descriptions and aliases; set status to ready or skipped; write a key into merge_into when two rows are the same thing. Export CSV and upload it here. Nothing is deleted on the wiki by an import.

      № 08 The bot

      Who writes to the wiki.

      A bot password made on the wiki (Special:BotPasswords) with the grants Edit existing pages, Create, edit, and move pages and High-volume editing. The password is stored encrypted with the site secret and never shown again; the site's own secret is the key.
